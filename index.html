Realcoins

Hayekian Realcoins and Prediction Markets

Toward currencies that stabilize purchasing power rather than dollars.

An exploration of whether prediction markets can function as more than digital casinos — and underpin a currency that is more store-of-value than existing options.

The Problem

Stablecoins stabilize dollars, not value

Stablecoins are ~$300B market cap, growing at a 50% 4-year CAGR. Almost all are USD-denominated.

If 10 apples cost $10 today and $20 tomorrow, your "stable" $10 has lost 50% of its value. Pegged to dollars ≠ pegged to purchasing power.

Can we build an instrument that stabilizes what those dollars actually buy?

Prediction Markets

From casino to information primitive

Prediction markets expand price discovery into domains traditional markets ignore: interest rates, wars, weather, rent.

This financialization of relevant information enables novel hedging strategies retail can access.

Hyper-localized prediction markets → personalized stores-of-value, with outcomes underpinning value. We call these realcoins.

Hayek's Lineage

The Denationalisation of Money (1976)

Hayek envisioned competitive private currencies, with the best at preserving value winning out — backed by a basket of commodities.

He identified the destination but lacked the mechanisms. Today three primitives change that:

  • Stablecoins — proven private digital money at scale
  • DeFi — programmable derivatives & structured positions
  • Prediction markets — liquid claims on real-world outcomes

Vitalik independently proposed an LLM-curated basket of prediction market shares matching user expenses.

Localized Insurance

Prediction markets ≈ insurance without the gatekeepers

If a market prices "Will your house burn down by January" at 1%, $200 buys 20,000 shares paying $20,000 if it does. Functionally identical to insurance — minus underwriters, claims processes, and "insurable interest" requirements.

Hedger vs. speculator: a renter experiences rent risk regardless. Buying shares offsets existing exposure, not creating new risk. They expect to lose value when outcomes go their way — purchasing power was preserved.

NYC Rent Example

Why this matters today

Avg Manhattan rent (Dec 2025): ~$5K/mo, up 6% from $4.7K in Dec 2024.

$4.7K covered 100% of rent in 2024 but only 94% in 2025 — a direct loss of experienced value.

A realcoin tied to Manhattan rent prediction markets would have risen with rent, preserving purchasing power.

System Architecture

How the parts fit together

Three external dependencies: a regulated issuer (custody + compliance), a prediction-market venue (Polymarket, Kalshi, Limitless), and a trusted outcome oracle (Zillow API, BLS, Chainlink-bridged).

On-chain components: ERC-20 realcoin token, NAV calculator, mint/redeem logic. The reserve is held off-chain by the issuer; on-chain attestations prove backing.

The user only ever touches the contract — deposit USDC in, get realcoins out; burn realcoins, get USDC back at current NAV.

Purchaser Flow

What a holder actually does, step by step

  1. Profile expensesUser connects bank/expense data, or selects a pre-built basket (e.g. "Manhattan renter — 1BR"). LLM or template maps spend categories → markets.
  2. Issuer constructs basketSelects markets per design (binary / parametric ladder / structured 60-40), sized to user's monthly exposure. Returns expected basis risk and worst-case loss.
  3. DepositUser sends USDC to the realcoin contract. Optional KYC depending on jurisdiction and ticket size.
  4. MintContract mints realcoin tokens at current NAV. Issuer routes deposit: T-Bills custodian + market-making desk that buys the prediction shares at quoted prices.
  5. HoldNAV updates daily as (a) T-Bill interest accrues, (b) market prices move with new information. User sees NAV ≈ tracking cost-of-living delta.
  6. Resolution eventOracle reports the underlying index (e.g., Zillow median Manhattan rent for Dec). Each market resolves Yes/No. Winning shares pay $1; losing shares pay $0.
  7. Reserve updatePayouts roll back into the reserve pool. NAV jumps if the hedge fired; NAV drops if it didn't (offset by T-Bill yield in design 3).
  8. Redeem (any time)User burns realcoins, receives USDC at current NAV. Held tokens earn the next cycle's hedge automatically — no re-entry.

Pricing & Position Sizing

The math the issuer runs at mint time

Shares purchased in a market priced at probability p with allocation $A:

shares = A / p

Each share pays $1 if Yes, $0 if No.

Allocation across rungs: the issuer sizes each rung so combined payouts roughly track the index. Higher-probability rungs get more capital (cover the likely path); lower-probability rungs get less (catastrophic-tail protection).

NAV calculation:

NAV = (Σ shares_i × mark_i + cash + T-Bill_value) / supply

where mark_i is the current market price of rung i's shares.

Lifecycle Timeline

What happens between mint and resolution

Realcoins are perpetual. Resolution doesn't end the position — payouts roll into the reserve and the issuer rebalances into the next cycle's markets. Holders only "check out" by burning tokens.

Oracle & Resolution

The hardest part of the stack

Markets are only as honest as their resolution source. Three failure modes to design against:

  • Oracle manipulation — pick sources with skin in the game (BLS official CPI, Zillow's published index, NYC RGB) and require multi-source consensus where possible.
  • Reporting lag — rent indices publish weeks after the period. Realcoin holders may want to redeem before resolution; NAV must reflect expected outcome via current market price, not just resolved outcomes.
  • Index drift / methodology change — if Zillow changes its methodology mid-period, payouts break. Lock the methodology in market terms; freeze methodology versions.

Settlement path: Oracle posts → market resolves on Polymarket/Kalshi → issuer's automated executor sells/claims winning shares for USDC → reserves credited → NAV recalc → next cycle's basket purchased.

Design 1

Binary Prediction Markets

Realcoin backed by Yes/No markets tied to cost-of-living thresholds. "Median Manhattan rent > $5K?" at 35% → $4.7K buys 13.4K shares paying out $13.4K if Yes.

Pros: simple, easy to price, already exist. A 5–10 market basket covers rent, groceries, electricity, healthcare.

Limitation — the gap problem: if rent rises $4.7K → $4.9K, purchasing power erodes but the hedge never activates.

Design 2

Parametric / Scalar Markets

Tie payouts to an index value, not a binary threshold. True continuous markets don't exist yet — approximated with nested binary "rungs".

Design 3

Yield-bearing collateral + prediction exposure

Combine 60% T-Bills (risk-free yield) with 40% prediction shares. If hedge fires, you keep purchasing power. If not, you still earn yield — better than fiat.

Payoff Curves Compared

How each design pays out as rent moves

Read: binary is cliff-shaped — total loss below threshold, full payout above. Parametric stair-steps to track the index. Design 3 lifts the entire curve by the T-Bill floor at the cost of capping upside.

The Open Problems

Specificity, liquidity, legality

  • Basis risk — national CPI doesn't track Manhattan rent. Specificity gap = hedge gap.
  • Liquidity — cost-of-living markets are thin compared to sports/elections; need natural counterparties.
  • Legal clarity — the prediction-market hedging layer isn't regulated like traditional structured products.
  • Counterparties — every Yes share needs a No-share holder. Issuers may seed liquidity initially but can't be the marginal counterparty long-term.

Hayek's insight wasn't that one currency would win, but that competition would discover which best preserved value. Realcoins are an entry into that competition. The tools exist today.
